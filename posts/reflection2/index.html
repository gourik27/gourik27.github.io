Reflection on Unit 2

February 4, 2026

Understanding the Nature of information problems through digital images.

Introduction:

In unit 2, The Nature of Information Problems, we explored how digital images represent information using numerical values. This unit helped me understand that images are not just pictures but structured data composed of pixel intensity values. It changed the way I look at images-from visual objects to mathematical representations.

What I learned:

I learned that a grayscale image is made up of pixels, and each pixel contains an intensity value that represents brightness. These values usually range from 0 (black) to 255 (white). By visualizing the pixel structure, I understood that every image is essentially a matrix of numbers.

Plotting a histogram of pixel intensities helped me see how brightness values are distributed across an image. It clearly showed which intensity levels were dominant and how the overall brightness of the image could be analyzed mathematically.

When we increased the brightness of the image and generated a new histogram, I observed how the intensity distribution shifted toward higher values. This practical comparison helped me understand how numerical operations directly affect visual output.

Observations and challenges:

Initially, I found it difficult to connect histograms with actual visual changes in the image. However, after comparing the original and modified histograms, I understood how brightness manipulation shifts intensity values. The lab activities made the concepts much clearer than theory alone

Conclusion:
