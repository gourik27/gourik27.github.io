The Y2K bug was a computer flaw, or bug, that may have caused problems when dealing with dates beyond December 31, 1999. The flaw, faced by computer programmers and users all over the world on January 1, 2000, is also known as the “millennium bug.” (The letter K, which stands for kilo (a unit of 1000), is commonly used to represent the number 1,000. So, Y2K stands for Year 2000.) Many skeptics believe it was barely a problem at all. This problem originated decades earlier , during 1960s-1980s, durin these periods the memory storage was expensive and limited. To save memory , programmers represented years using only 2 digits instead of 4 digits . For example “1999” was represented as “99”. It worked nicely till 1999 but when year changed to 2000 it created a critical flaw.Bacause computers considered 00 as 1900 not 2000 . Many systems were heavily dependent on date calculations and this posed a significant risk to global infrastructure. It was primarily a software and design failure.
